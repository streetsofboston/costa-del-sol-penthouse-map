Compliance assessment · Google Play Payments policy

Stripe-only in your Android app: not compliant as it stands

Your setup — subscriptions sold to US users, paid only through Stripe inside a Google Play–distributed app, with no enrollment in any Google program and nothing reported to Google — violates Google Play's current Payments policy. US developers may use Stripe in-app, but only inside Google's alternative billing program: enrollment, the alternative billing APIs, transaction reporting within 24 hours (required since October 1, 2026), and a 10% service fee on auto-renewing subscriptions.

Assessed October 26, 2026 Scope: Google Play distribution · US users · auto-renewing subscriptions Based on Google's own policy pages and developer guides, read in full
Verdict

Not compliant — on two independent grounds

The arrangement fails the Payments policy twice over. It does not use Google Play's billing system, which §2 requires by default — and it does not qualify for the §8 alternative-billing exception either, because that exception is expressly conditioned on enrolling in Google's program and accepting its terms. Since October 1, 2026 it also breaches the program's reporting duty and fee obligation, which now apply to every authorized US transaction.

Nothing about this requires abandoning Stripe. The fix is to bring the existing Stripe flow inside the program — enroll, integrate the APIs, report, and pay the fee — or to restructure into one of the compliant shapes set out in §7.

Your current setup, gate by gate
1 · Program enrollment Not met §8 makes enrollment a condition of offering any in-app alternative billing. You are not enrolled in the US alternative billing program.
2 · Alternative billing API integration Not met No Play Billing Library alternative-billing integration, no Google information dialog before the Stripe flow. Google enforces this technically: an unenrolled account gets BILLING_UNAVAILABLE.
3 · Transaction reporting within 24 hours Not met Required for enrolled US developers since October 1, 2026 — every authorized transaction: sign-ups, renewals, upgrades, downgrades, refunds, even $0 free-trial starts. You report nothing.
4 · Service fee — 10% of subscription revenue Not met Auto-renewing subscriptions carry a 10% Play service fee under the program, payable since October 1, 2026, on top of Stripe's own processing fees.
01 — THE REASONING

Why "the program is opt-in" doesn't cover you

The two pages you linked seem to point in opposite directions: the October 2025 US update says Google no longer requires its own billing, and the program page calls enrollment "opt-in." Read together with the Payments policy itself, they don't. The injunction removed the mandate to use Google Play Billing; it did not remove the conditions on using something else.

The default rule (§2) and the steering ban (§4)

"Play-distributed apps requiring or accepting payment for access to in-app features or services, including any app functionality, digital content or goods (collectively 'in-app purchases'), must use Google Play's billing system for those transactions unless Section 3, 8 or 9 applies."

"Other than the conditions described in Section 3, Section 8 and Section 9, apps may not lead users to a payment method other than Google Play's billing system."

Payments policy §§2, 4 — support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9858738

Subscription services are expressly listed among the in-app purchases §2 covers. A Stripe-only app does not use Google Play's billing system, so it survives only if an exception applies.

The only in-app exception (§8) is conditioned on enrollment

"Developers serving users in eligible countries/regions may offer users an alternative billing system within the app if they enrol in the applicable programme and agree to the additional terms and programme requirements."

Payments policy §8 — same page

"Developers participating in this program must comply with the following requirements: — Only offer alternative billing systems within the app by implementing and enrolling in this program"

Offering an alternative billing system for users in the United States — support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16497028

What "opt-in" actually means

"No, this is an opt-in program. If you do not wish to offer US users an alternative to Google Play's billing system, no action is required."

Program FAQ — same program page

The choice Google offers is binary: use Google Play Billing exclusively (no action needed), or offer an alternative such as Stripe — in which case enrollment, API integration, reporting, and the fee all attach. There is no third state of "alternative billing without the program." Your app currently sits in exactly that non-existent third state, so it satisfies neither §2 nor §8.

The US update page says the same thing in its own words. It confirms Google "will not require the use of Google Play Billing in apps distributed on the Google Play Store, or prohibit the use of in-app payment methods other than Google Play Billing" — and immediately conditions the practical route: developers may offer alternative billing "by complying with the following policies and programs (as launched on December 9, 2025)."

US injunction policy update — support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/15582165
02 — THE FIX

What compliance requires, keeping Stripe

The program page frames these as mandatory steps for anyone who wants to offer alternative billing ("If you would like to offer alternative billing, you must complete the following steps").

  1. Review the program requirements

    Eligibility: an app or game on mobile or tablet form factors offering alternative billing to users in the US and its territories.

  2. Enroll in Play Console

    Settings → Alternative billing: complete the onboarding steps and enroll in the US alternative billing program, accepting the additional program terms.

  3. Integrate the alternative billing APIs

    Per the API integration guide — Play Billing Library 6.1+, the "alternative billing only" flow, and Google's information dialog before every Stripe checkout (details in §3).

  4. Configure each app in Play Console

    Opt each app in or out of the program and provide subscription management URLs.

  5. Report every transaction, and pay the fee

    Since October 1, 2026: report all authorized US transactions within 24 hours via the alternative billing APIs, and pay the applicable service fee — 10% for your subscriptions.

Ongoing program duties

  • PCI-DSS compliance if you handle card data directly (with Stripe's hosted elements this burden is largely Stripe's, but the program requirement is stated unconditionally).
  • Comply with applicable payments and minors laws.
  • Provide customer support and a dispute process for unauthorized transactions.
  • Provide links for order history, subscription management, and refund requests.
  • If Google Play Billing is also offered, all users must be able to access it consistently and reliably.
Program page, requirements and enrollment steps — answer/16497028
03 — THE REPORTING DUTY IN DETAIL

How reporting actually works

Reporting is a backend obligation, not an in-app one — and it's the piece most unlike anything Stripe asks of you. The decisive line from Google's integration guide:

"All transactions made through an alternative billing system must be reported to Google Play by calling the Google Play Developer API from your backend within 24 hours, providing an externalTransactionToken… A new externalTransactionToken should be generated for each one-time purchase, each new subscription, and for any upgrade/downgrades to an existing subscription."

In-app integration guidance for alternative billing only — developer.android.com/google/play/billing/alternative/…
In the app: Billing Library integration
  • Use Play Billing Library version 6.1 or higher — the alternative billing APIs don't exist in earlier versions.
  • Initialize BillingClient with enableAlternativeBillingOnly(), connect to Play, and confirm availability with isAlternativeBillingOnlyAvailableAsync.
  • Before the Stripe flow, every time, show Google's information screen — it tells the user billing will not be managed by Google Play — by calling showAlternativeBillingOnlyInformationDialog. Proceed only on BillingResponseCode.OK.
  • Enrollment is enforced by the API itself: a BILLING_UNAVAILABLE response means the transaction "shouldn't proceed under this program… or your account has not been successfully enrolled in the program."
  • Obtain the reporting token in-app via createAlternativeBillingOnlyReportingDetailsAsync (returns externalTransactionToken) and pass it to your backend.
From the backend: the externaltransactions API

Report through the Google Play Developer API's externaltransactions endpoint after a payment has been authorized. Per the backend guide, this "applies to all transactions, including initial charges, renewals, refunds, and others" — including "$0 transactions resulting from free trial purchases."

  • Initial transaction (including a subscription's first charge): the externalTransactionToken is required in the request body, alongside pre-tax and tax amounts, transactionTime, and userTaxAddress.regionCode. Each report also carries a developer-generated unique externalTransactionId as a query parameter — it must contain no personally identifiable information. For subscriptions, set externalSubscription.subscriptionType to RECURRING (auto-renewing) or PREPAID.
  • Renewals: the token is not needed. Send a new unique externalTransactionId for each renewal, with the initial transaction's ID in the initialExternalTransactionId field.
  • Upgrades / downgrades: reported like a new purchase on the same endpoint, sending the externalTransactionToken provided for that transaction.
  • Quota: 1,200 queries per minute on the create/refund external-transaction endpoints.
  • Reports for ineligible countries or unenrolled accounts are rejected server-side — you cannot report your way into compliance retroactively without enrolling first.
Backend integration guidance — developer.android.com/google/play/billing/outside-gpb-backend
Refunds and cancellations — the two easy ones to get wrong
  • Refunds are reportable. Use the refundexternaltransaction endpoint, referencing the externalTransactionId "of the specific recurrence of the subscription that is being refunded." Refunding an entire subscription means a separate refund request per transaction — the initial charge plus every recurrence. Full and partial refunds are supported (a partial refund requires the refunded pre-tax amount).
  • Cancellations are not. The guide defines no cancellation-transaction report — a cancellation authorizes no payment. Cancellation matters only on re-purchase: send a new external transaction ID "if the recurring transaction is canceled or expired and the same product is bought again later."
04 — THE MONEY

The fee: 10% on subscriptions, whatever the tier

The US program's fee table has a new-vs-existing-install split (cutoff: June 30, 2026) and a first-$1M tier — but for auto-renewing subscriptions none of that moves the number. It is 10% in every row.

Tier Auto-renewing subscriptions Other digital — new installs Other digital — existing installs
First $1M annual earnings 10% 10% 10%
Standard service fee 10% 20% 25%
Play Games Level Up / Apps Experience 10% 15% 20%

Source: US alternative billing program page (answer/16497028), read October 26, 2026.

  • New installs: a transaction from a user whose first install or first update from Google Play occurred on or after June 30, 2026. Existing installs: before that date — June 30, 2026 is "the regional rollout date serving as the effective date." For a subscriptions-only app this distinction is academic; it bites only on non-subscription digital goods (20% vs 25% standard).
  • Stripe's own processing fees are separate and additional — Google's pages don't address them.
  • Context, secondary Under Google's March 2026 global fee model, reporting describes the 10% as a service fee, with an additional 5% billing fee charged only when Google Play Billing is used — alternative billing avoids the billing fee but not the service fee. This split is not stated on the US program page itself.

The March 2026 settlement did not change any of this — and the court never approved it

On March 4, 2026, Google and Epic entered a new settlement and asked the US District Court to enter a revised "Modified Injunction" (RPMI) that would have capped Google's service fees at 9% or 20% (depending on transaction type and install date) through 2032. On July 15, 2026, the parties withdrew that joint motion. Google's own page says only that "our current policies and programs remain in effect," and Google has confirmed it continues to comply with the original October 2024 permanent injunction — which remains the governing US instrument.

Sources conflict Several July 2026 headlines said the companies "abandoned the settlement." MacRumors corrected its story: the commercial settlement is intact and Google is lowering fees voluntarily, but the court order embodying it was never entered. Any March 2026 coverage saying the settlement "was approved by the court" is wrong. Either way, the RPMI would not have eliminated enrollment or reporting — and Google switched both on, October 1, under its own program terms after the withdrawal.

In flux Industry commentary contends the October 1 fee schedule is Google's unilateral position, not court-approved, and may be challenged before Judge Donato, who retains compliance oversight. No court ruling on the October 1 fees was found in this research — treat the fees as payable now, but not necessarily settled law.

06 — EXPOSURE

What Google can do about the current build

There is no US-program-specific penalty schedule; Google enforces through its general Play enforcement process. An unenrolled Stripe-only build is a Payments-policy violation on its face, so the realistic first outcomes are rejection of the build or removal until a compliant version ships — with escalation for persistence.

  1. Rejection

    A new app or update "will not be made available on Google Play." No impact on account standing.

  2. Removal

    The app and all previous versions "are removed from Google Play and will no longer be available for users to download," restored once you submit a policy-compliant update. Users "may not be able to make any in-app purchases… until a policy-compliant version is approved." Removals don't immediately affect account standing, "but multiple removals may result in a suspension."

  3. Suspension

    For "egregious or multiple policy violations, as well as repeated app rejections or removals." The APK/bundle can no longer be used, and suspensions "count as strikes" — "multiple strikes can result in the termination of individual and related Google Play Developer accounts."

  4. Restricted account

    All apps in the catalog removed; no publishing until "you have fixed all policy violations."

  5. Account termination

    "All apps in your catalog will be removed from Google Play and you will no longer be able to publish new apps… any related Google Play developer accounts will also be permanently suspended… Any new account that you try to open will be terminated as well." Google's general rule: "Repeated or serious violations… will result in termination of individual or related Google Play Developer accounts."

No grace period covers non-enrollment

The only transition relief Google documented was for reporting and fees ("once required" → October 1, 2026) and, for external-content-links downloads only, the February 1, 2027 deadline. Neither excuses being unenrolled. No amnesty for unreported pre–October 1 transactions was found — reporting simply wasn't required before that date. Enforcement actions apply in all regions unless otherwise stated, and stand unless an appeal is granted.

Enforcement Process — support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/9899234
07 — THE FIVE SHAPES AN APP CAN TAKE

Scope distinctions that change the answer

Your app is shape (a). Shapes (d) and (e) are the only routes that avoid Google's program entirely — and both require giving something up.

(a) Stripe inside the app

You are here — non-compliant

"Alternative billing within the app" → Payments §8 + the US alternative billing program. Enrollment, API integration, 24-hour reporting (since Oct 1, 2026), and the 10% subscription fee are all required. Offering Stripe alongside Google Play Billing instead of instead of it does not change any of these duties — only the integration flow differs.

(b) Native Stripe and native Google Play Billing

Compliant — same program as (a)

Both payment options presented natively in the app — the "user choice" model. The alternative billing program covers alternative billing offered "in lieu of or alongside" Google Play Billing, so this is the same enrollment as shape (a); Play Billing itself needs no enrollment. The duties split by route: Stripe transactions carry 24-hour reporting and the 10% service fee, exactly as in (a); Google Play Billing transactions are handled entirely by Google, which deducts its 15% all-in fee — nothing to report. Play Billing must be accessible to all users "in a consistent and reliable manner" — a genuine option, not a decoy. The commercial case for this shape, and the differential-pricing arithmetic, are covered in §8.

(c) Link out to a web checkout

Different program — still enrollment

The external content links (ECL) program — a separate enrollment under Payments §9, completed before using any links, with its own APIs. Fees apply to transactions or installs completed within 24 hours of following the link: recurring subscriptions 10%; app downloads are a fixed fee — $2.85 per install for apps ($3.65 for games). All transactions must be reported, including $0 free-trial starts; transaction reporting runs from Oct 1, 2026, but download reporting is deferred to February 1, 2027. Conflict Google's earlier Sept 17, 2026 notice set that download deadline at December 1, 2026; the Oct 21 update supersedes it. Note: an in-app button or webview to a Stripe checkout page is ECL, not a loophole — §4 expressly lists "in-app webviews, buttons, links…" as leading users to another payment method. And ECL is link-out by definition: there is no native-screen variant of it — the moment payment happens inside the app, the app is in shape (a) or (b), not (c). You may enroll in both programs at once.

(d) Buy on the web, use in the app

Compliant — no program, no fee

The consumption-only ("reader") model genuinely changes the answer: no Google Play Billing, no alternative-billing enrollment, and no service fee on web sales — provided nothing can be purchased from within the app. Google: "Google Play allows any app to be consumption-only, even if it is part of a paid service. For example, a user could log in when the app opens and access content paid for somewhere else." Inside the app you may not link to the web checkout, but non-link informational text is expressly allowed — e.g. "Go to our website to upgrade your subscription to Premium" — and outside the app (email marketing and other channels) you may promote alternative purchase options freely. The moment the app itself offers the purchase or links to checkout, it falls back into (a), (b) or (c).

(e) Don't distribute via Play

Outside the policy entirely

The Payments policy governs "Play-distributed apps." The same APK sold through other Android stores, your website, or preloads owes Google no enrollment, reporting, or fee: "Android gives developers the freedom and flexibility to distribute their apps on other Android app stores, directly from a website, or through device preloads, all without using Google Play's billing system." Caveat: channels linked to Play can pull an app back into Google's net — ECL download fees, and Google's new third-party-store / Play Catalog Access arrangements, under which apps distributed through Play's catalog still carry Google's service fee.

ECL program — answer/16470497 · Payments explainer (reader apps, off-Play distribution) — answer/10281818
08 — ADDING GOOGLE PLAY BILLING ALONGSIDE STRIPE

The “user choice” model: no compliance relief, real commercial advantages

The US program expressly covers alternative billing offered “in lieu of or alongside” Google Play Billing. Adding Play Billing (IAP) as a second checkout option is therefore permitted — but it does not dilute, offset, or remove any of the duties assessed elsewhere on this page. Enrollment in the alternative billing program, 24-hour reporting, and the 10% service fee apply to every Stripe transaction exactly as if Play Billing were not offered, and you additionally build and maintain a second billing integration. What adding IAP buys is commercial and operational, not regulatory.

1 · Conversion: one tap, no card form

Play Billing checkout completes with the payment method already stored on the user's Google account — no card details to type into a third-party sheet inside the app. A real slice of users will not enter card details that way, and for them the Stripe-only paywall is a dead end. Play balance, gift cards, and carrier billing are also available only through Google's checkout. Offering Play Billing alongside Stripe lets card-willing users keep the Stripe route while card-unwilling users can still subscribe.

2 · Google as merchant of record — on Play Billing sales only

On sales routed through Play Billing, Google handles sales tax / VAT collection and remittance, payment fraud, chargebacks, and dunning — grace periods, account hold, and payment-retry logic. On Stripe sales, all of that remains your own burden: tax registration and remittance in the jurisdictions where you owe it, fraud and chargeback handling, and your own failed-payment recovery. The tax piece alone is a genuine operational load for a subscription sold across many jurisdictions.

3 · Built-in subscription lifecycle

Play Billing comes with Google's subscription machinery built in: account hold, pause, resubscribe, proration, and managed price changes, plus subscription management inside the Play Store, where users already manage their other subscriptions. On Stripe, the equivalent lifecycle — holds, pauses, win-back, proration, price-change consent — has to be built or bought. This applies only to subscriptions sold through Play Billing; Stripe subscriptions still run on your own lifecycle tooling.

4 · It must be a genuine option, not a decoy

“If Google Play Billing is also offered, all users must be able to access Google Play Billing in a consistent and reliable manner.”

Program requirement — Offering an alternative billing system for users in the United States — support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/16497028

A buried, broken, or deliberately degraded Play Billing option does not satisfy the program. If you offer it, it has to work for every user, every time.

Differential pricing: permitted — and the arithmetic behind it

Google “will not require a developer to set a price based on whether Google Play Billing is used.”

US injunction policy update — support.google.com/googleplay/android-developer/answer/15582165

There is no price-parity requirement in the US, so the Play Billing route may be priced higher than the Stripe route. Whether it should be — and by how much — is a matter of arithmetic. Under Google's current US fee structure, a subscription sold through Play Billing costs a 10% service fee + 5% billing fee = 15% all-in, with payment processing included. The alternative route costs the same 10% service fee and no billing fee, but bears Stripe's own processing on top — typically around 2.9% + $0.30 per charge. Worked on a $100 subscription:

  • Stripe route, at $100: $100 − 10% Google service fee − ~2.9% Stripe processing − $0.30 ≈ $87 net.
  • Play Billing route, at the same $100: $100 − 15% all-in = $85 net.
  • Play Billing route, at +15% ($115): $115 − 15% = $97.75 net — roughly $11 more per subscriber than the Stripe route.

The true cost gap between the two routes is therefore about 2 points, not 15: a premium of roughly +2% on the Play Billing route would equalize your net revenue across both checkouts. A +15% premium over-recovers — it is permitted, but it functions as steering: at that gap most users will choose Stripe, and you earn more from the few who don't. Price it knowingly, as a steering decision, not as cost recovery.

The three-way choice, restated

(a) Stripe only

Cheapest net

Full program duties — enrollment, API integration, 24-hour reporting — plus the 10% service fee and Stripe processing. No second integration to maintain, and the best net per subscriber, provided your paywall converts card-willing users well.

(b) Both — user choice

Same duties + IAP benefits

The same program duties and 10% on every Stripe sale, plus a Play Billing integration to build and maintain. In return: one-tap conversion for card-unwilling users, Google as merchant of record, and the built-in lifecycle — on Google-routed sales only, at 15% all-in.

(c) Play Billing only

Only variant with no program

The only configuration that eliminates the alternative billing program entirely: no enrollment, no 24-hour reporting, no external-transaction APIs. The cost is the 15% all-in fee on every sale and the loss of your Stripe funnel, pricing control, and direct billing relationship.

Bottom line: if your Stripe paywall already converts well and your subscribers are card-willing, adding IAP buys you nothing you need. Its case rests on conversion you are currently losing, and on offloading tax, fraud, chargeback, and lifecycle work for the sales Google routes — never on compliance.

09 — IF YOU HAVE EEA USERS

The EEA is the same architecture, enforced earlier and harder

For EEA users the structure is identical in kind — alternative billing is a DMA-driven, opt-in-if-you-offer-it program with mandatory Play Console enrollment, program terms, and API integration — but Google has enforced it earlier and continuously: EEA developers have had to integrate the alternative billing APIs since March 13, 2024 and to "report to Google Play all authorised transactions from users in the EEA within 24 hours" with no "once required" deferral, and service fees were payable throughout (before June 30, 2026, the without-user-choice fee was the standard fee reduced by 3%; from June 30, 2026 the EEA table converges with the US one — 10% for auto-renewing subscriptions at every tier, 20%/25% for other digital goods on new/existing installs, 10% on the first $1M). The EEA program adds an eligibility condition the US page lacks: the developer "must be a registered business." Link-outs in the EEA are likewise a separate enrolled program — the External Offers Program, distinct from US ECL — whose June 4, 2026 terms charge a 0% initial acquisition fee plus an ongoing 10% (subscriptions) / 20% (other) fee and €1.20 per app download for transactions and installs within 24 hours of the link. In short: if any of your subscribers are in the EEA, the "we report nothing" position has been non-compliant there since 2024, not just since October.

10 — HONEST UNCERTAINTIES

Open questions this research could not close

  • January 28, 2026. The date the December 2025 changes "came into force" is consistently reported in secondary sources (including a listed company's annual-report summary of Google's announcement), but no current Google page found states it. Google's live pages present enrollment simply as a standing condition.
  • Will the October 1 fees and reporting survive court scrutiny? Google presents them as program terms; industry commentary calls them unilateral and unapproved. No court ruling was found. In flux.
  • The commercial settlement's exact legal status — as distinct from the withdrawn motion — is reported inconsistently ("abandoned" vs "intact"); no court docket document was read directly in this research.
  • Injunction expiry. Secondary reporting (Mondaq) puts the injunction-era policies' end at November 1, 2027; Google's page says only that changes apply "during the effective dates of the injunction." Not confirmed from the injunction text itself.
  • User-choice variant. The US program covers alternative billing "in lieu of or alongside" Google Play Billing; the alongside (user-choice) integration guide exists, but its US-specific enrollment nuances were not separately verified.

Confidence is high on the core verdict, the enrollment/reporting duties, and the fee table — all rest on Google's own pages, with the two anchor pages you supplied read live on October 26, 2026. The settlement narrative (medium confidence) is the part resting on reputable secondary reporting.
